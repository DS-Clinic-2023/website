1. What is Eczema?
‍

Eczema, also known as atopic dermatitis, is a chronic, inflammatory skin condition characterized by dry, itchy, and red skin. It is a common condition that affects people of all ages, but it is most often diagnosed in infants and young children. The exact cause of eczema is not fully understood, but it is believed to be a combination of genetic and environmental factors.


2. What are the causes of Eczema?
‍

Common causes of eczema include:

  • Genetic predisposition: People with a family history of eczema, asthma, or hay fever are more likely to develop the condition.
  • Environmental factors: Exposure to irritants, such as harsh soaps or detergents, and allergens, such as dust mites or animal dander, can trigger or worsen symptoms of eczema.
  • Immune system dysfunction: People with eczema may have an overactive immune response to certain triggers, leading to inflammation and itching.
  • Dry skin: People with eczema often have dry skin, which can make the condition worse.


3. What are the symptoms of Eczema?
‍

Symptoms of eczema can vary from person to person and can range from mild to severe. Some common symptoms include:

  • Itchy, dry, and scaly skin
  • Red, irritated patches of skin
  • Small, raised bumps or blisters that may ooze or crust over
  • Cracked or thickened skin
  • Dark, discolored patches of skin


4. What is the care plan for Eczema?
‍

Treatment of eczema typically includes a combination of self-care measures and medication. A proper skin care plan is important to keep the skin moisturized and reduce inflammation. This may include:

  • Keeping the skin clean and moisturized by using mild soap and applying a fragrance-free moisturizer right after bathing
  • Avoiding triggers, such as harsh soaps or detergents, and allergens, such as dust mites or animal dander
  • Applying medicated creams or ointments as directed by a healthcare provider
  • Oral or injectable medications, such as antihistamines, may be used to reduce itching

It is also important to seek medical attention if symptoms worsen or if signs of infection develop. In some cases, oral or injected medication may be necessary for more severe cases.
‍

It is important to see a dermatologist if you have symptoms of eczema as they can help you identify triggers, create a skin care plan, and provide appropriate treatment.
